\title{Dengue Surge Warning for Singapore:\\
Can Recent Surveillance and Weather Data Flag Elevated Risk?}
\author{Tran Duy Tai \and Do Nhat Thanh \and Nguyen Thi Quynh Thu}
\date{42.510 Statistical Learning}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Can recent dengue counts and weather identify weeks with a materially elevated risk of a dengue
surge in Singapore? We forecast 1-, 2- and 4-week-ahead surges in weekly dengue fever (DF)
notifications from the Ministry of Health bulletin (2012--2022, 574 weeks), with lagged rainfall
and temperature from NEA/MSS Changi station and reanalysis humidity. Surges are defined in three
ways (above a rolling 52-week mean + 2 SD, above the same-season 90th percentile, or a rise of at
least 25\% and 50 cases), plus onset-only versions that exclude weeks already above the threshold.
Seasonal and persistence baselines are compared with logistic, lasso, CART, random forest, Poisson
and ridge count models in weekly expanding-window rolling-origin forecasts (target weeks
2016--2021), with block-bootstrap intervals. 2022 is reported separately as a final-period check.
For level surges, persistence already reaches PR-AUC 0.93--0.97 and no model beats it reliably.
Count regression improves growth surges (PR-AUC 0.26 vs 0.17) and seasonal onsets (0.89 vs 0.26,
but only 5 events). Weather adds no measurable skill beyond recent counts. These are predictive,
not causal, results; few outbreaks, missing serotype data and prior exposure to 2022 limit them.
\end{abstract}
