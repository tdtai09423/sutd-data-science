\section{Methods and validation}
\label{sec:methods}
Let $y_t$ denote weekly DF cases and $h\in\{1,2,4\}$ the forecast horizon. We label a
surge if (A) $y_{t+h}>\mu_t+2\sigma_t$, where $\mu_t,\sigma_t$ use the previous 52
weeks; (S) $y_{t+h}$ exceeds the 90th percentile for dates within $\pm17$ days of the
same season in earlier years, excluding observations from the previous 26 weeks; or
(G) $y_{t+h}\ge(1+p_h)y_t$ and $y_{t+h}\ge50$, with $p_h=0.25,0.30,0.45$. A-onset and
S-onset are defined only when $y_t$ is not already above the corresponding threshold.
Thresholds use information available at $t$.

Predictors include log-transformed current and lagged counts, recent growth and rolling
means, seasonal terms, and rainfall, rain days, temperature and humidity lagged by
0--12 weeks. We compare prior, seasonal-rate and persistence baselines with logistic
regression on cases, weather or both; L1-regularised logistic regression with forward
time-series tuning; a depth-3 CART; random forest; Poisson regression; and ridge
regression on log counts. Count forecasts are converted to probabilities of exceeding
the target using a log-normal predictive distribution based on training residuals.
Model comparisons use PR-AUC and Brier score; alarms at probability $0.20$ illustrate
a missed-surge:false-alarm cost ratio of 4:1, not a clinically validated threshold.

We use expanding-window rolling-origin forecasts by week from 2016, refitting every
week and purging training rows whose complete label horizon would not yet be observed.
The development period is defined by target weeks before 2022; target weeks in 2022
form the final-period evaluation, including forecasts issued in late 2021 for 2022.
During this rolling evaluation, earlier 2022 labels can enter training for later
forecasts only after they would have become observable.
We report PR-AUC, precision, recall, alarms per year and Brier score/calibration;
count-model errors are also reported as MAE/RMSE in cases per week. Uncertainty uses
500 moving-block bootstrap draws with 26-week blocks. A one-week reporting-delay
sensitivity re-runs the primary one-week targets with counts from week $t$ unavailable.

\textbf{Holdout qualification.} Earlier exploratory runs pooled forecasts through 2022
before the final split was selected. In addition, the rolling evaluation updates using
earlier observed outcomes within 2022. Thus this is a final-period prequential check,
not a strictly untouched static test set or independent external validation.
