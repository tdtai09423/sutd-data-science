\section{Background and motivation}
Dengue is endemic in Singapore with large epidemic years. An early signal that cases
will rise sharply in the coming weeks would help the National Environment Agency and
hospitals act before the peak. Previous Singapore studies used weather and past cases
to forecast incidence \citep{hii2012forecast,shi2016three}.

\section{Objectives}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Build a reproducible weekly national dataset of dengue cases and weather (2012--2022).
  \item Define and compare two operational surge definitions (threshold exceedance and growth).
  \item Predict surges 1, 2 and 4 weeks ahead with logistic regression, count regression and tree-based models.
  \item Select models with time-respecting validation and assess the added value of weather.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Data}
\begin{itemize}
  \item MOH Weekly Infectious Disease Bulletin, data.gov.sg (dengue fever and DHF, 2012--2022).
  \item Open-Meteo historical weather archive (rainfall, temperature, humidity), daily.
  \item Supporting: NEA dengue clusters (spatial snapshot), NEA humidity records, OpenDengue \citep{clarke2024opendengue}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodology}
\paragraph{Targets.} Binary surge indicators at horizons $h\in\{1,2,4\}$ weeks under
two definitions; count of cases $y_{t+h}$ for regression models.
\paragraph{Predictors.} Lagged cases and growth, seasonality, weather lagged 0--12 weeks.
\paragraph{Models.} Logistic regression (with $\ell_1$ selection), Poisson regression,
decision tree, random forest, gradient boosting.
\paragraph{Validation.} Rolling-origin cross-validation by year with purging; ROC-AUC,
PR-AUC, Brier score, recall and false alarm rate.

\section{Expected outcomes}
A comparison of surge definitions and models, an estimate of the useful warning lead
time, and an interpretable model (coefficients / tree rules) that could support decisions.

\section{Risks}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Case data on data.gov.sg ends in 2022: extend from NEA/CDA bulletins or OpenDengue if needed.
  \item Few epidemic years means few positive cases: use PR-AUC, simple models, regularisation.
  \item Reporting and behaviour changes (e.g.\ 2020 COVID-19 period, Wolbachia releases): sensitivity analysis.
\end{itemize}
